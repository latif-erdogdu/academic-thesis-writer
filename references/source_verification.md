# Source Verification Guide

## Purpose
Ensure all sources used in the thesis are credible, accessible, and appropriate for academic work.

## Verification Checklist

### For Every Source
- [ ] **Author credibility**: Is the author qualified/expert in the field?
- [ ] **Publication venue**: Is it peer-reviewed, published by a reputable publisher?
- [ ] **Date recency**: Is the source current enough for the topic?
- [ ] **Accessibility**: Can the full text be accessed legally?
- [ ] **Citation integrity**: Are citation details complete and accurate?

### Credibility Indicators
#### Strong Signs (Include)
- Published in peer-reviewed journal
- Published by university press or academic publisher
- Author has relevant credentials (PhD, professor, researcher)
- Institution affiliation is clear
- DOI assigned
- Cited by other reputable sources

#### Weak Signs (Use with caution or exclude)
- Self-published blog post without review
- No author listed
- Publisher unknown
- Contains excessive advertising
- Only available through paywall without access
- Multiple URLs pointing to same content

### Red Flags (Exclude)
- Wikipedia as primary source (use original source instead)
- Predatory journals
- Sites with clear commercial bias affecting content
- Outdated information for time-sensitive topics
- Unverifiable claims or statistics
- Broken links or missing pages

## Source Categories

### Primary Sources
- Original research papers
- Government reports
- Census data
- Archival materials
- Direct observations/experiments

### Secondary Sources
- Review articles
- Textbooks
- Academic books
- Commentary on primary sources

### Tertiary Sources (Use sparingly)
- Encyclopedias
- Dictionary entries
- Summary websites

## Verification Process

### Step 1: Initial Assessment
```markdown
Source Name: [Link/Title]
Publication Date: YYYY-MM-DD
Author: Name, credentials
Publisher: Organization name
URL: Full URL with DOI if available
```

### Step 2: Quality Checks
- Check for peer review statement
- Verify publisher reputation
- Confirm author affiliations
- Review citation history
- Assess potential biases

### Step 3: Cross-Reference
- Search for citations to this work
- Compare with other authoritative sources on same topic
- Check if source is cited in major reviews/textbooks

### Step 4: Accessibility
- Can you access the full text?
- Is there a DOI that resolves?
- Are there paywall restrictions?

## Common Pitfalls

### Avoid These Mistakes
1. **Chaining citations**: Always find and cite original source
2. **Over-relying on secondary sources**: Seek primary materials
3. **Ignoring publication date**: Field-specific recency needs vary
4. **Accepting preprints uncritically**: Note as "unpeer-reviewed" if applicable
5. **Missing citation elements**: Ensure all required fields are complete

### Special Considerations
- **Conference papers**: May be peer-reviewed but verify venue quality
- **Working papers**: Mark as preliminary; seek final published version
- **ArXiv preprints**: Note status clearly in citations
- **Grey literature**: Document why it's appropriate despite limitations
